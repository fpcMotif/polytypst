\chapter{Measures and Their Spaces}
\section{Outer measure}
For a set $E\subset\mathbb{R}$ we write $m^*(E)$ for its outer measure,\origpage{142}
and the fraction $(a+b)/(c+d)$ stays on one line in running text.
Lebesgue's construction starts from intervals, whose length is the difference of their end points.
\begin{equation}
  m^*(E) = \inf\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^\infty \ell(I_k) : E\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^\infty I_k\Bigr\}
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Carathéodory]
The family of $m^*$-measurable sets is a $\sigma$-algebra, and $m^*$ restricted to it is a measure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $A$ and $B$ measurable. The sum splits over $A$ and its complement.
\end{proof}
